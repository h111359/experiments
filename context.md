## 1.3 Operating Model (DevOPS)

The same team covers all four pillars — there is no separation between development and support capacity. All members are capable of fulfilling any role and can shift quickly in case of urgency.

| Pillar | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **D** — Development | Building new features and enhancements per roadmap and backlog |
| **O** — Operations | Maintenance, technical procedures, deployment automation, CI/CD |
| **P** — Production Management | Monitoring, change management, security, reliability |
| **S** — Service | Technical requests, incident management, audit and support cases |

### Benefits

- **Effectiveness** — incidents resolved faster due to deeper system knowledge
- **Motivation** — engineers strive for quality knowing they will support what they build
- **End-to-end perspective** — support experience directly informs future development decisions
- **Dynamic capacity allocation** — effort shifts rapidly between support and development as needed
- **Knowledge retention** — no separate team to train; onboarding is accelerated through production exposure
- **Technical ownership** — team is empowered to take technical decisions and drive product roadmap, planning, and vendor management

### Delivery Process (High Level)

1. Intake & triage
2. Solution shaping & architecture proposal
3. Backlog refinement & estimation
4. Iterative development & code reviews
5. Automated deployment & validation
6. Monitoring & incident response
7. Continuous improvement feedback loop

### Extensibility

The model supports adding external resources (consultants or vendors) to boost capacity temporarily or permanently while keeping knowledge and control internal. This is enabled by:

- Self-serve onboarding processes
- Standardized product documentation templates
- Established development and deployment conventions

## 1.7 Strategy

### Products

- **Global Data Products Catalogue** — centralized discoverability of data products with business/technical owners and access procedures
- **AI-Ready Documentation** — detailed domain and technical documentation stored in a machine-readable format
- **Knowledge Internalization** — know-how, decision-making ability, and control are maintained internally

### Teams

- **Technical Ownership** — each product has a single team as its dedicated technical owner
- **Own Way of Work** — teams are autonomous in defining their processes, principles, and roles
- **DevOPS Model** — Development, Operations, Production Management, and Service all covered by the owning team
- **Extendable** — external resources can augment capacity while the team retains knowledge and control

### Governance

- **Data Owner Assignment** — each data domain has an appointed owner responsible for lineage, quality, access, and certification
- **Centralized Infrastructure Owner** — infrastructure managed by a single organization guaranteeing common standards and processes
- **Aligned Product Roadmaps** — users and stakeholders involved in prioritization and requirements gathering
- **Wholistic Data Catalogue** — data discoverable with semantic description, data model, ownership, confidentiality classification, and physical location
- **Quality Certification Process** — uncertified data must not be treated as a source of truth; certified data must be trusted
- **Code Repositories** — all production code stored in GitHub, complying with D&A TEC standards for quality, reusability, and documentation

### Infrastructure

- **Azure-First** — approved tools catalogue covering cloud and local tools, defined by role
- **Architecture Templates** — pre-approved architectures with recommended services and documented variations; custom solutions remain possible for edge cases
- **Centralized Infrastructure Support** — D&A teams are not responsible for infrastructure management; this is delegated to Cloud teams
- **Sandbox Environments** — each team equipped with a sandbox for experimentation, training, and ideation
- **Shared Codebase** — common architecture templates and code assets across teams

## 2.1 Ideas for improvement of the team (confidential)

### Already tried ideas

#### Zero-Friction Setup Day

##### Description

Dedicate 2 hours for the entire team to install GitHub Copilot in VS Code and accept their first suggestion. No training deck. No theory. Just install, open a real project file, and start typing. The Accenture study showed 81.4% of developers installed Copilot the same day they got a license and 96% accepted a suggestion immediately.

##### Outcome

The team had the task and most already have VSC installed
A framework for AI development is created - AIB and videos were created to demonstrate how to work with it
Many team members were not motivated and not active on this task

#### The "AI-First Ticket" Rule

##### Description

For every new user story or service request, require a 1-line field: "How will AI assist with this task?" It can be as simple as "Copilot for boilerplate SQL" or "ChatGPT for regex generation." This builds the habit of *thinking about AI* before coding.

##### Outcome

We have tried but the people fake it. They find excuses or do it just because they are asked and do not continue further exploration of AI. Still it will be monitored more - this initiative should remain.

#### AI for Documentation Generation
The team maintains product documentation, OneNote pages, and onboarding materials. Use Copilot Chat or ChatGPT to generate first drafts of technical documentation from code, then review and refine. This alone can save hours per sprint.

##### Outcome

AI Builder framework covers documentation generation

#### 10. AI-Powered SQL and DAX Generation
Your team works heavily with SQL, T-SQL, and DAX. These languages have extremely predictable patterns — exactly where Copilot excels. Start with: "Write a DAX measure that calculates year-over-year growth for net sales revenue." The time savings will be immediately visible.

##### Outcome

AI Builder framework covers documentation generation

#### 11. AI for ETL/ELT Pipeline Boilerplate
Synapse Spark notebooks, ADF pipelines, and Azure Functions all involve repetitive patterns. Use Copilot to generate logging, error handling, and self-healing boilerplate. This directly accelerates the D (Development) pillar.

##### Outcome

AI Builder framework covers documentation generation

#### 12. AI for Test Writing
GitHub's research identifies test writing as one of Copilot's strongest capabilities. Set a team norm: "For any new function, ask Copilot to generate the initial test suite." This improves quality while reducing the most tedious development task.

##### Outcome

AI Builder framework covers documentation generation

#### Identify 3–5 AI Champions

Select 3–5 enthusiastic early adopters (across both Global D&A Engineering and CCL D&A CoE). Give them a "champion" role. Their job: use AI tools daily and share one concrete win per week in the team channel. GitHub's official adoption guide recommends this as the single most effective pattern. Good candidates: engineers already comfortable with automation and scripting.

##### Outcome

Tried even further - to form teams and all in GDATD to contribute to AI idea. Very few people were active. Most did something, but if not pushed - they do not progress.

#### AI Show & Tell at Tech Forum
You already have a Tech Forum. Add a 15-minute standing slot: "AI Demo." One team member shows a real task they completed faster with AI. Rotate presenters. This creates gentle accountability and knowledge transfer without extra meetings.

##### Outcome

This practice is encouraged by default

#### Copilot Chat Cookbook Lunch & Learn (30 Minutes) + Prompt Engineering Workshop
Host one 30-minute session walking through the GitHub Copilot Chat Cookbook (official GitHub resource). Focus on the 5 most relevant recipes for your tech stack: SQL generation, code explanation, debugging, test writing, and documentation.

##### Outcome

### To be tried

#### AI-Assisted Code Reviews
Before submitting a PR, ask Copilot Chat to review the code: `@workspace /review`. This catches issues faster and teaches the team that AI is a co-reviewer, not a replacement. It fits into the existing code review workflow with zero extra meetings.

#### AI for Service Requests and Incident Triage
Your team handles technical support and incident management. Use AI to draft initial investigation notes, summarize error logs, or generate root-cause hypotheses. There should be set of prompts/instructions which to be executed each time a Dynamics issue or service request is created. This initial plan should be documented and reviewed for each incident or request.

#### "AI Win of the Week" in Team Channel
Create a recurring MS Teams post (every Friday) where anyone can share an AI success. "Copilot wrote my entire test class in 3 minutes" or "ChatGPT helped me debug a Databricks error in 5 minutes instead of 45." Social proof is the most powerful motivator.

#### Pair Programming with AI as the Third Partner**
Your team already values collaboration. Formalize "AI-Assisted Pair Programming" — two humans + Copilot. One person drives, one reviews, Copilot suggests. This makes AI adoption social rather than individual, which removes the "I don't have time to learn alone" excuse.

#### Celebrate AI Adoption, Not Just Output
In team reviews or retrospectives, explicitly call out and recognize team members who adopted AI tools effectively. Engineering leader recognition changes behavior faster than mandates.

#### The 15-Minute Daily AI Challenge
For 2 weeks, issue a daily micro-challenge via Teams: "Use Copilot to write a Python function that validates an NSR data file." "Ask ChatGPT to explain a regex." 15 minutes max. This builds muscle memory without disrupting project work.

#### Curated Learning Path (Not Open-Ended)
Don't tell people "go learn AI." Give them a curated 3-hour track with a specific path
